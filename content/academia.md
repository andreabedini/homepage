[[extra.research]]
n = "01"
title = "Critical phenomena in lattice polymers"
tag = "Statistical mechanics"
figure = "images/models.svg"
body = '''
My most recent work studied self-avoiding trails — paths on a lattice that may
revisit a vertex but never re-use an edge. Looking at their critical behaviour
turned up some surprises: the collapse of trails in three dimensions shows a
non-standard scaling that no theory yet predicts, and the phase diagram shifts
in interesting ways once you add stiffness or nearest-neighbour interactions.
The same intersecting-path geometry underlies problems as far afield as the
Anderson metal&ndash;insulator transition, and characterising the collapse
transition of trails is still considered an open problem in the field.
'''

[[extra.research]]
n = "02"
title = "Exact calculation of graph polynomials"
tag = "Algorithms"
body = '''
I built an algorithm for the Tutte polynomial of a graph that extends the
classic transfer-matrix approach with the computer-science idea of
tree-decomposition. The result was two-fold: a
<a href="https://github.com/andreabedini/tutte">software package</a> faster
than anything else available (published through Zenodo, DOI
<a href="http://dx.doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.15941">10.5281/zenodo.15941</a>),
and a general framework that adapts to many other graph polynomials — letting
researchers state and test conjectures about chromatic-polynomial roots in a
fraction of the time.
'''

[[extra.research]]
n = "03"
title = "Supersymmetric methods in combinatorics"
tag = "Mathematical physics"
figure = "images/hyperedge.svg"
body = '''
My PhD used a fermionic (Grassmann) formalism to count forests on graphs and,
more generally, hypergraphs. I proved that the asymptotics depend on the ratio
of trees to vertices, giving rise to a phase transition — one that turns out to
be the spontaneous breaking of a hidden supersymmetry, and the appearance of a
&ldquo;giant component.&rdquo;
'''
