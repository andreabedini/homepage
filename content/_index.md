I have a PhD in theoretical physics and a few years of research behind me,
and I'm still an analytical mind at heart: whatever the job title, a lot of my
work has been data analysis, simulation and computation. I'm based in Perth,
Australia, and I work remotely for [Tweag](https://tweag.io) as a software
engineer — these days mostly making build systems and developer tooling a
little less painful.

I've been playing with computers since I was a teenager — early enough to have
watched the world change around them. I learned to program in BASIC when I was
ten, and my first paid job was as a Linux system administrator. A dozen
languages later, Scheme and Prolog are still on the list.

Since then: solution and cloud architecture, data science and engineering, and
now software engineering. I learned Haskell because it let me say what I
meant more precisely, and that led me on to dependent types, working through
Software Foundations and Programming Language Foundations in Agda. Off the
clock I solder — mostly resurrecting my kids' toys. I like learning things,
which is why I fall into deep rabbit-holes, and why I have far too many books
still to read.

I'm one of the organisers of the [Perth Functional Programmers Meetup](https://www.meetup.com/PerthFP/),
which has been quiet since COVID.
Back when I was in Melbourne, I started a science-based story-telling show
called [The Laborastory](https://thelaborastory.com).
